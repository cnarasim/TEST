# Supply chain strategy

## Three goals for the supply chain
### Available
- The right product in every store and order
- Fewer gaps at peak and at launch
### Fast
- Same-day or next-day for most guests
- Pickup ready within [n] hours
### Efficient
- Lower cost to serve per unit
- Less stock for the same service

## The network from brand partner to guest
Layout: retail network
- Brand partners: prestige and mass vendors
- DCs: regional distribution centers
- Fast fulfillment: e-commerce orders
- Stores: pickup and ship from store
- Guests: home delivery or pickup

## The trade-offs we choose on purpose
| Trade-off | Our choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Service or cost | Speed where guests value it | Pay for speed that sells |
| Stock or availability | Deep on top sellers, lean tail | Stock where it earns its keep |
| Central or local | Central plan, local execution | Scale with local fit |
| Own or partner | Own DCs, partner last mile | Control with flexibility |
| Efficiency or resilience | Two sources for key items | No single point of failure |

## Six capabilities we must build
Layout: hexagons
- Demand sensing: forecasts by store and day
- Inventory visibility: one view of every unit
- Automation: DC picking and sortation
- Transport: better routes and fuller loads
- Brand collaboration: shared plans and data
- Resilience: dual sourcing and risk alerts

## The supply chain roadmap by quarter
Layout: timeline quarters
- Demand sensing pilot: Jan 2027 to Jun 2027 (Planning)
- DC automation, phase 1: Mar 2027 to Dec 2027 (Distribution)
- Ship from store, all stores: Apr 2027 to Sep 2027 (Stores)
- Transport optimization: Jul 2027 to Dec 2027 (Logistics)
- Peak readiness: Oct 1, 2027 (Supply chain)

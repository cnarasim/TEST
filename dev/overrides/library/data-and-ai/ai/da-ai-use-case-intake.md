# AI use case intake and prioritisation

## What AI intake covers
### Purpose
- One front door for every AI idea
- The best few funded first, on evidence
### In scope
- Idea, value, data, feasibility and risk
- Ranking, funding and the pilot plan
### Outcome
- Fewer scattered experiments
- Pilots with a clear measure of success

## Five steps from idea to pilot
Layout: process chevrons
- Submit: problem, users and the expected value
- Size: value, effort and data readiness
- Rate: risk tier and the controls it needs
- Rank: value against feasibility
- Pilot: scope, measure of success, end date

## Value and feasibility rank the ideas
Layout: matrix
- Do now: high value, ready data - pilot this quarter
- Prepare: high value, data not ready - fix data first
- Quick help: lower value, easy - use standard AI tools
- Park: lower value, hard - revisit next year

## Every idea gets a risk tier
| Tier | Example | Controls needed |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Drafting internal text | Standard tools, user review |
| Medium | Guest-facing answers from our content | Evaluation, monitoring, disclosure |
| High | Decisions about people or money | Human approval, bias testing, legal review |

## The AI council funds the pilots
Layout: swimlane handoffs
- Business team: Submits the idea
- AI team: Sizes value, data and effort
- AI team: Ready for a pilot?
- AI team: If yes, recommends it to the council
- AI team: If no, gives the gaps to close
- AI council: Funds and sets the success measure

## KPIs that show it works
| KPI | What it measures | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas triaged | Ideas sized within [n] weeks | Intake portal |
| Pilots with measures | Pilots with a success measure | Portfolio tool |
| Pilots scaled | Pilots that reach production | Portfolio tool |
| Value delivered | Benefit vs the business case | Benefits tracking |

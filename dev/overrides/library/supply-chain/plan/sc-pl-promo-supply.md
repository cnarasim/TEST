# Promotion and event supply

## What promotion supply covers
### Purpose
- Stock on hand for the whole event
- Little left over when it ends
### In scope
- Lift forecast and early purchase orders
- Pre-positioning in DCs and stores
### Outcome
- Promoted items stay in stock
- Leftover stock planned, not marked down

## The event plan in five phases
Layout: process phases
- Plan: lift forecast and buy (T-12 weeks)
- Buy: early POs and vendor commits (T-10 weeks)
- Position: DC and store stock in place (T-2 weeks)
- Sell: daily sales and refills (event weeks)
- Close: leftovers returned or rebalanced (T+2 weeks)

## Merchandising plans, supply delivers
Layout: swimlane handoffs
- Merchandising: Shares the event and items
- Demand planning: Forecasts the lift by item
- Supply planning: Raises early purchase orders
- Vendor: Confirms quantity and ship date
- Supply planning: Confirmed in full?
- Supply planning: If yes, plans store deliveries
- Supply planning: If no, finds a substitute item

## Systems and data for event supply
| Step | What the system does | Records it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Event plan | Holds event dates, items and offers | Promotion calendar |
| Lift forecast | Adds expected lift to base demand | Event forecast |
| Early buy | Orders event stock ahead of time | Purchase orders |
| Positioning | Plans DC and store event stock | Allocation, transfer orders |
| Review | Compares sales with the forecast | Event results |

## KPIs that show it works
| KPI | What it measures | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Event in-stock | Promoted items on shelf | Store stock snapshots |
| Lift accuracy | Forecast vs actual lift | Event forecast vs sales |
| Sell-through | Event stock sold by the end | Event sales vs supply |
| Leftover value | Stock left after the event | Inventory after the event |

## Today and target
### Today
- Event stock bought late, at higher cost
- Leftovers marked down after every event
### Target
- Event supply locked ten weeks out
- Leftovers sent to stores that sell them

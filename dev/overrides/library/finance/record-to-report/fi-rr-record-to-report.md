# Record to report

## What record to report covers
### Purpose
- Turn every transaction into reliable results
- One version of the numbers
### In scope
- Journal entries, reconciliations and close
- Consolidation and management reporting
### Outcome
- Financial statements on time
- Results explained by driver

## From transaction to board report
Layout: swimlane handoffs
- Subledgers: Post sales, purchases and stock
- General ledger: Books journals and accruals
- General ledger: Accounts reconciled?
- General ledger: If yes, close the period
- General ledger: If no, investigate the difference
- Consolidation: Eliminates intercompany
- FP&A: Publishes the results

## Systems and data for record to report
| Step | What the system does | Records it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| One ledger | Finance and management accounting in one journal | General ledger |
| Journals | Manual journals with review and approval | Journal entries |
| Reconcile | Subledgers reconciled to the ledger | Reconciliations |
| Consolidate | Entities combined, intercompany eliminated | Consolidated results |
| Report | Financial statements and management reports | Reports |

## Controls and exceptions
| Control | How it works | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Journal approval | Manual journals reviewed by value | Controller |
| Account ownership | Every GL account has an owner | Controller |
| Intercompany match | Intercompany balances agree before consolidation | Consolidation |
| Report sign-off | Results signed by the CFO before release | CFO office |

## KPIs that show it works
| KPI | What it measures | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| Days to report | Month end to published results | Reporting calendar |
| Manual journal share | Journals booked by hand | Journal source |
| Unreconciled balance | Value of unexplained differences | Reconciliations |
| Restatements | Results corrected after release | Reporting log |

# Decks

Part of the ULTA Presentation Central instructions: SKILL.md's rules for every deliverable, token rules and checks apply here too. What each slide can be is in guides/deck-layouts.md.

## Decks
1. The first cell (`GUIDES = ['decks', 'deck-layouts']`) printed `build_deck.py inventory`: every slide and layout by number and name with what it takes, then the template infographics (vision house, OGSM, funnel, big numbers, donut and more) in plain words. Pick from all of it, not only the classic slides. `build_deck.py inventory --full` only when a slide needs named slots.
2. **Plan like a presentation expert, then write the storyboard.** Before any JSON, settle four things:
   - **The answer and the ask.** The deck's answer in one sentence, and what the audience must do with it (decide, approve, align, know). A leadership or steering-group deck opens with the answer (an At a glance slide from `"glance"`, or the headline numbers) and ends with the ask (the action table (12) with owners and dates, and a `"decision"` bar).
   - **The storyline** that fits the purpose, each part one or more slides. Decision or proposal: answer and ask, why now (the problem in numbers), the option and why, the value (big numbers, a chart), the plan (a timeline), the risks (the risk table), the ask. Status update: the headline numbers against target, progress (status roadmap), what changed (charts), issues and risks, decisions needed, next steps. Strategy: the vision (vision house, OGSM), where we are, priorities, roadmap, KPIs (scorecard), asks. Business review: KPIs against target, trends, drivers, actions. Process or architecture: the picture first (flow, swimlane, diagram), then how it works and what changes. More than 12 content slides: chapters with dividers and an agenda that names them.
   - **The picture each message needs** - never tiles or a table when the content is: 2-4 figures → big numbers (`headline-metrics`, `quad`) with a context line each · 5+ figures in one unit → a chart (`lollipop`, bars; over time `trend`; against a target `bullet`, `gauge`) · shares of a whole → `donut` (up to 5), `treemap`, `waffle` · before and after → big numbers with "72% to 80%" context, `dumbbell` or `slope` for 3+ · dates, milestones, phases → a timeline · steps in order → the template's flow (20) or a process flow (chevrons, a cycle, phases, a pipeline with its conversion; `"icons": "auto"` for pictograms) · a supply chain, a DC or cross-dock, online orders or a retail finance process (procure to pay, three-way match, month-end close) → a retail diagram or starter, first (below); who does what → a swimlane · systems and data flows → a diagram · KPIs against target → `scorecard` · strategy → `priorities` (vision house) or `ogsm` · risks with mitigations → the risk table (10); actions with owners and dates → the action table (12) · 2-6 ideas without numbers → tiles or cards · one statement → `big-note` on a photo. Pictures on at least half of the content slides; never three plain-text slides in a row; each slide tells its story another way than the slide before it, unless it carries that slide on (below).
   - **Retail first.** A message about how goods, orders, documents or money move - the supply chain (vendors, DCs, stores, guests), inside a DC or a cross-dock, how an online order reaches the guest, returns, store replenishment, procure to pay, a three-way match, order to cash, the month-end close - goes on a retail diagram or starter (`{"retail": ...}`, guides/deck-layouts.md) or a process flow with its pictograms (`"icons": "auto"`) before any other layout: never tiles, a list or the plain flow (20). The build sets it itself where the slide's items fit as they are, every word kept, and says so in a note: points or a flow naming vendors, DCs, stores and guests in order become a supply chain network, a DC's zones in order a DC flow, documents in order a document flow, the ways an order reaches the guest the fulfilment paths, the steps of a retail process a flow with pictograms (a process flow gets its pictograms); never when the slide would then repeat a slide beside it more than it does. The EXPERT `retail:` lines name the rest (a starter its headline names, a shape to fill). `"retail_first": false` on a slide or the deck keeps it as written; supply chain risks, KPIs or a table stay the risk table, big numbers or the table.
   - **Context on the slide:** a `"summary"` line under the headline where the slide's figures need a so-what, context lines under big numbers, a `"decision"` bar where the slide asks for something; the narrative in the notes. When the deck comes from a document or pasted notes, put its path in `"source_doc"` (save pasted text as `/mnt/data/source.md` first): the build lists the source's figures that reached the slides, only the notes, or nothing.

   Then write `storyboard.json` with the short keys:
```json

 {"source":1,"text":["Customer portal launch"]},
 {"source":6,"headline":"Self-service cuts calls by 18%","text":["Automated inquiry triage absorbs 42K contacts","Store associate app flags open order status"],"notes":"Executive Narrative: Self-service capabilities deflected 18% of call center volume in Q3 by resolving tier-1 tracking inquiries at intake. Strategic Rationale: Frees guest services agents for high-value consultation.  {"diagram":["Web portal → API gateway: orders","API gateway → Order service: order"],"style":"zones","headline":"Every order passes the API gateway"},
 {"source":32,"text":["Thank you!"]}]}
```
   - **What a slide can be** - the template's layouts by name, modern charts, project timelines, swimlanes, process flows, retail diagrams and starters, the template's own infographic slides, chart my data and every diagram style, with what each takes: `guide('deck-layouts')`, read before writing the storyboard.
   - `text` fills the text boxes in reading order (empties the rest); `table` fills the slide's table (`null` keeps a cell); `slots` takes named slots from `--full`. A list of 2-6 points: `{"bullets":["Lead: detail",…],"headline":"…"}` - drawn as numbered tiles (never a text slide), each point written "Lead: detail" with a 1-3 word lead and at most 15 words in all (`"plain": true` keeps a classic list); a picture: `{"picture":"/mnt/data/x.png","headline":"…"}` (both may be combined).
   - Diagram styles need the right shape of flows: tree = one root system everything hangs from; chevron = one chain A → B → C; hub = one system most flows touch; tech = system names that say their kind (… App, … Gateway, … DB, … Queue, a person); cake = layers as a top-to-bottom CHAIN, each layer with its points - write it as `"diagram": {"nodes": [{"id": "ch", "label": "Channels", "items": ["ulta.com", "Stores"]}, …], "flows": [{"source": "ch", "target": "om", "label": "orders"}, …]}` (two layers fed by the same layer merge into one tier).
   - Diagram text: system names 1-3 words, line labels 1-3 words (about 20 characters, e.g. `A → B: JSON intent`), detail goes in the notes.
   - **Every slide reads at three depths - glance, scan, read - and stays a slide, never a page of text.**
     * **Glance: the headline.** The slide's one message as a takeaway, at most 45 characters.
     * **Scan: the summary line and the body.** `"summary"`: one sentence under the headline that adds the evidence or the "so what" in the slide's own numbers ("Accuracy rose from 72% to 80% in the pilot stores; markdowns fell 1.3 pts"), at most 24 words (about 15 set one line), never the headline again. The body stays short: cards 6-15 words, numbered tiles at most 15 words, table cells at most 8, big numbers a number, a 3-6 word label and, when it helps, a third line of context of up to 6 words (`"+8 pts\nForecast accuracy\n72% to 80%"`). A slide that asks for something: `"decision": "Decision: approve the pilot scope in November"` (or `"Ask: …"`, `"Next step: …"`), one sentence of at most 24 words, set in a light bar above the footer.
     * **Read: the speaker notes (`"notes"`).** The narrative, rationale, data nuances, background and risks live here, never as paragraphs on the slide. The summary and the decision open the notes too ("Takeaway: …"), so a presenter has them in Presenter View.
     * The build sets the summary line and the decision bar only where the layout has clear space (charts, diagrams, timelines, swimlanes, process flows and retail diagrams make room for them); on a layout without room they stay in the notes and an ADVICE line says so. Covers, dividers, photo slides and approved library slides take neither. A number the slide and its notes do not hold (no new facts), more than 24 words, or a summary that needs two lines where its layout holds one FAILs the build.
     * **Pre-read decks** (sent ahead, read without a presenter, a leave-behind): `"mode": "read"` at the top of the storyboard. The build adds an **At a glance** slide after the agenda from `"glance"` - 3-5 points, each "Lead: detail" in 15 words or fewer, with `"glance_headline"` as its takeaway (default "At a glance") - or, without it, from the headlines; and it names the content slides that still lack a summary: give every content slide one. Presenting (the default, `"mode": "present"`): a summary where the slide needs one; `"glance"` on its own also adds the slide. A storyboard with its own "Executive summary" or "At a glance" slide never gets a second one.
   - **Dense sources (PowerPoint only):** when the input is a detailed business document (BRD, architecture spec, strategy memo > 300 words), keep every slide crisp and carry the detail in the slides' speaker notes (`"notes"`), with an appendix section for reference tables, so no context is lost. A PDF handout or an e-mail summary of the finished deck: `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/audience.py export /mnt/data/out/<Name>.pptx --pdf --email`, run after the build printed READY.
   - **Word limits the build enforces:** card slide (7) = a first row of 2-3 column titles (1-3 words each), then 1-2 rows of cards, each a 1-4 word title + a 6-15 word line · action table (12) what 4 / description 14 / when-who 5 · hypothesis-action (10) 14 a cell · grid (14) headers 3 / cells 8 · big-number labels 3-6 and one context line of up to 6 · any other table 12 words a cell and 120 in all · points 2-6, at most 18 words each and 80 in all.
   - **No text dumps (the build FAILs them):** points go in cards (7) or `bullets`, never as lines stacked in a text box; a process is a flow slide (20) or a chevron diagram, never "A → B → C" typed as text; slide 6's two boxes take ONE statement each (6-30 words, one sentence, not a list of actions - a list is cards (7)), and slide 6 is used at most once a deck; no single layout for more than half the content slides; an architecture slide shows the landscape (6-8 systems; 8 is the most one diagram slide holds), not 3-5 boxes in a strip.
   - **Two slides in a row never look alike (the build FAILs them):** each slide gets the layout its content needs, and the next one a different look. Looks: headed columns (cards 7, `paired-columns`, `four-pillars`, `objectives-scope`) · numbered tiles (`bullets`) · big numbers (9, `quad`, `headline-metrics`) · tables (10, 11, 12, 14, `period-scorecard`, a `table`) · the flow (20) · each template infographic. By content: steps, phases, waves or a lifecycle → the flow (20), a chevron diagram or a timeline; a set of 2-6 items → tiles or cards; items compared on the same points → a table; numbers → big numbers or a chart. Charts, diagrams, graphics, timelines and photos are their own pictures. When two slides meet that look alike or tell their story one way, the build re-lays one of them itself when every word fits and neither neighbour has that look or way - points that run in order as a flow (a 1-3 word label and 2-6 words a step), headed columns beside another way as tiles (15 words a tile) - and says so; the rest FAIL with the layouts that would hold them.
   - **Two slides in a row never tell their story the same way, unless the second carries the first on (the build FAILs them).** The ways: a set of points (cards, pillars, focus areas, scope columns, tiles, a list, two text boxes) · steps in order (a flow, a process flow, a funnel, a chevron or sequence diagram, a DC, cross-dock or document flow) · who does what (a swimlane in any style) · a timeline (a gantt, a roadmap, milestones, the journey, every timeline style) · big numbers (KPI cards, `quad`, `headline-metrics`, `kpi`, rings, a gauge) · KPIs against target (the scorecards, `bullet`) · a trend over time (a line or area, columns by period, `trend`, `stream`) · items ranked by size (bars, columns by item, `lollipop`, `bubbles`, `rose`) · shares of a whole (`donut`, a pie, 100% bars, `waffle`, `treemap`, `sunburst`, `sankey`) · a side-by-side comparison (`paired-columns`, `dumbbell`, `slope`, `butterfly`, `radar`, the fulfilment paths) · how the parts fit together (system diagrams, a pyramid, a target, a venn, the vision house, an OGSM, a supply chain network) · a 2x2 · a table · a map · the people · a picture. So a ranking after a ranking (bars, then a lollipop), tiles after cards, two timelines or two sets of big numbers in a row FAIL, with the ways the second could tell its story (never one its neighbours use) or the merge of the two. A slide that carries the one before on says so: "(cont.)", "(2/3)" or "(2)" in its headline, or `"continues": true`, and shares its subject (the same labels, table header or data, or headline words): a long table over two slides, the next page of KPI cards, as-is then to-be of the same systems. A numbered list carried over carries on by itself; covers, agendas, dividers and statements end a run. A deck that shows one kind on purpose (every chart type, every style) is a catalogue: `"gallery": true`. Check a storyboard before the build: `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/storyline.py storyboard.json` lists each slide's way and the pairs that repeat one.
   - Slides: 1 cover · 2 agenda (title "Agenda"; up to 5 items as `"table":[[null,"Item"],…]`, no header row; numbers are automatic) · 5 divider · 6 two text boxes · 7 three columns × two points (table: column titles, then 2 rows of "Sub-headline\n\nsentence") · 9 two or three big NUMBERS only (row 1 = 1-2 word headers, never left as "Headline"; row 2 cells "78%\nshort label", an optional third line of context of up to 6 words - sentences there are rejected; use 7 for text) · 10 hypothesis→action (middle column empty) · 12 action table · 14 grid (row 1 = `[null, five column titles]` - the template's sample headers fail; then one row per category, cells of at most 8 words) · 20 flow with `"steps":[{"label","text"}]` (3–6; label = 1-3 words for the top band, text = 2-6 words, never a sentence) · 32 closing; the layout names work too (`build_deck.py inventory`). Other brands: `CATALOG.md`.
   - Typography follows the template's slide 3 standard, applied and checked on every build: headlines 36 pt and highlighted text 24 pt in the display face, sub-headlines Circular Bold 18 pt, body Circular Regular 12 pt. The standard names Chronicle Deck Semibold as the display face; this skill uses **Playfair Display Black as a stand-in** (as the template's own slides do; owner decision - `brand.json` → `typography.display_font` switches it). So say "follows the template's type scale, with Playfair as the Chronicle stand-in" - never claim an exact Chronicle match. Never set fonts or sizes yourself. Because body text is always 12 pt, fill a layout to fit it: two-text-box slide (6) for one statement of 6-30 words a box, once a deck (set automatically as a 20 pt statement, centred), bullets for 2-6 points (drawn as tiles with the template's card type: 18 pt lead, 14 pt detail); with less to say, use cards (7) or a big-number slide (9) instead of leaving a big box mostly empty.
   - Look: bold is for headlines, card titles and the big number only - body sentences are regular weight in dark text (the builder enforces it); one idea per card in 6-15 words; numbers on slide 9 always carry a 3-6 word label (`"74%\ntoken savings per deck"`) and may add a short context line (`"74%\ntoken savings per deck\nfrom 52% in Q1"`). Slide 7 card = `"Sub-headline\n\nsentence"` (the blank line matters).
   - **Colours: zero deviation.** Every colour in every file is a template colour (the template's colour wheel, both theme palettes, its tints and neutrals); status green / amber / red go on marks only, never on text. The build maps strays and `tools/brand_colours.py <file>` FAILs any that remain - never pick colours yourself, and never ask for a colour outside the template.
   - Rules: no full stop at the end of headlines, titles or card titles (the builder removes them); one message per slide; the headline is that message as a takeaway, in 45 characters or fewer (over 60 fails the build) - 'Returns down 18% since self-service', never a topic label such as 'Returns' or 'Decisions requested' (labels fail the build; Agenda, Appendix and Q&A titles are fine); one text per box (an empty box fails: pick a layout with fewer boxes; a value with no box fails too, so nothing is dropped); never leave sample text; never invent numbers, dates or owners, and no "illustrative" or "hypothetical" figures either (write "-").
3. Build and look in one cell (one turn): it fills the template, fits the text, makes diagrams native, then checks, labels and stamps, and prints one status line plus problems only, then the **EXPERT REVIEW** (story, pictures, context, the source's figures, type too small to read in a room) with an EXPERT line for each thing that would make the deck better; the contact sheet (every slide in one picture) comes back in the same turn for your review. Every build, small or big, runs as a tracked job in the same sandbox that the cell watches for up to 45 seconds, so no cell runs into the chat's time limit:
```python
r = tool('job.py', 'start', 'deck', '--wait', '45', '--', 'python', f'{SKILL_DIR}/tools/build_deck.py', 'build', 'storyboard.json', '--out', '/mnt/data/out/<Name>.pptx'); print(r)
for line in r.splitlines():
    if line.startswith('PICTURE '): show(line[8:].strip())
```
   `DONE deck` and then the build's own output: most decks, big ones too, finish within the 45 seconds. `RUNNING deck`: the build goes on - in the next cell run the same lines with `r = tool('job.py', 'wait', 'deck')`, again while it prints RUNNING. Never start a build that is still running again (BUSY): if a cell stopped early, the job may still be running - `wait`, don't restart. The same for **data over 50,000 rows** (`dashboard.py build`, `chart_advisor.py --build`): `job.py start <name> --wait 45 -- python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/<tool>.py ...`. A second build of a file still being built is refused (BUSY): wait for the first. A build that did not finish leaves no deck at its `--out` path (decks are made out of sight and put there only when finished): no file there means it did not finish - run it through `job.py`, never send a file from any other folder. Never copy, rename or send a file before its build printed READY or NEEDS CORRECTION, and never change a built file in Python; if you did change one anyway, run `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/ooxml_check.py <file>` and send it only on PASS (otherwise rebuild).
4. **Fix before you deliver.** SHRUNK HARD, STILL TOO LONG, a word break ("word wider than its box") or "text cut off" means the text doesn't fit that slot - never deliver text ending in '…'. Shorten it or move it to a roomier slide, then rebuild (up to 2 rounds). Never deliver a file with word breaks. **Template placeholder text (Lorem ipsum, Text here, Sub-headline, Headline, Insert headline, XX%) is never delivered, not even as a DRAFT:** a build that leaves any writes no deck at all (`NOT WRITTEN`; the work copy marked INCOMPLETE in the `_validation` folder is never sent). Give that slide what its layout takes (`build_deck.py inventory <source>`) or drop the slide, then rebuild. Still NEEDS CORRECTION after 2 rounds: deliver it as DRAFT and name the slides. **Act on the EXPERT lines in the same rounds:** give each named slide the picture it names, add the opening answer, the ask or the takeaway lines it asks for, put each source figure it names on its slide (the takeaway line, a context line under a big number, a callout) and give each part of the source it names a slide or a takeaway line, then rebuild; keep a slide as it is only when the user asked for that layout, and say so in one line. `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/deck_expert.py storyboard.json [--source <document>]` gives the same review before a build.
5. Attach the .pptx and any `.drawio`. Tell the user they can edit it in PowerPoint like a hand-made deck, or ask for changes here in chat. Give the final EXPERT REVIEW in one line ("pictures on 9 of 12 content slides, the answer up front, the ask at the end").
- **A PDF or a non-branded deck → rebuild it on the template:** `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/convert_deck.py <file.pdf|.pptx> --out /mnt/data/out/<Name>.pptx --build`. It copies titles, bullets, tables, chart data, pictures and notes onto template slides (nothing invented) and prints one line per slide; three figures with labels become the big-number slide (9), a 3 x 2 grid of titled points the card slide (7), a numbered agenda table the agenda slide, a single row of stage names a chevron process and two short text boxes two tiles, so the source's design survives. Fix only the `FIX` and `FAIL` lines: edit those slides in `<Name>-storyboard.json` (e.g. shorten a headline), then run build_deck on it. A portrait PDF is a document: use doc_outline (WORKFLOWS A).
- A deck already on the template but made elsewhere (hand-made, older version), or one whose headline wraps into the content in PowerPoint: `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/fix_deck.py <deck>` before delivery. A deck from v139 or earlier with bar, column or pie charts that PowerPoint offers to repair ("found a problem with content"): the same command renumbers its chart axes and it opens clean; editing it here does that too. A deck that shows template placeholder text (Lorem ipsum, Sub-headline) cannot be fixed in place: rebuild it with `convert_deck.py <deck> --out <new>.pptx --build`, which reads the deck's own words, leaves the placeholders out and keeps its classification.


## Other ways to show a slide (existing deck)
"Show me other ways to present slide 5", "make slide 3 more visual": real alternatives, the user picks, only that slide changes.
```python
opt = lambda *a: print(subprocess.run([sys.executable, f'{SKILL_DIR}/tools/slide_options.py', *a], capture_output=True, text=True).stdout)
opt('show', DECK, '--slide', '5')                   # what the slide holds (text, numbers) + layout ideas; a chart: options written
opt('show', DECK, '--slide', '5', '--more')         # a chart slide, the user wants others: the next 3, none repeated
opt('preview', DECK, '--slide', '5', '--options', '/mnt/data/out/options.json', '--out', '/mnt/data/out/slide5-options')
opt('apply', DECK, '--session', '/mnt/data/out/slide5-options', '--choice', 'B', '--out', '/mnt/data/out/<Name>-v2.pptx')
```
0. **A chart slide** (its numbers are in the chart): `show` reads the chart's data and writes the options file itself (ranked lollipop, bars, share donut, big numbers, sized circles, wedges, columns, a table - from the real values) and prints `OPTIONS <file>`: preview that file as it is. "Other ways" / "don't like these": `show ... --more`, then preview the new file. When it prints NO MORE OPTIONS, say every way to show these numbers has been offered. **Never sketch, draw or describe options yourself and never use placeholder values** - the only pictures of options are `choices.png` from preview.
1. Any other slide: write 2-3 options (4 at most) in `options.json`, each one storyboard slide from guides/decks.md - a layout, template infographic, timeline, chart or diagram - holding the same words and numbers in a different form: `{"options": [{"id": "A", "label": "Four headline metrics", "slide": {"source": "headline-metrics", "headline": "…", "table": [[…]]}}, …]}`. Choose layouts that suit the content; never pad the choice with weak ones, and never the way a slide beside it already tells its story: `show` names those ways ("beside it: ...") and leaves them out of its own options and ideas, and `preview` refuses an option told one of them (unless its headline marks it "(cont.)").
2. `preview` builds and checks each option: one that drops or changes a number, or drops words, is refused with the reason (fix it or leave it out); on a chart slide every group and value of the chart must be on the option (a headline on a photo is refused). It writes `choices.png` - the slide now beside the lettered options. `show()` it, name the options in one line each, and wait for the user's pick.
3. `apply` swaps in the pick: only that slide changes (the tool confirms the other slides are byte-identical), speaker notes stay, and the original file stays as it was for undo. Attach the new file; end with the RESULT line.

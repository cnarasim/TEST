## Infographics and one-pagers - a guided conversation
"Infographic", "one-pager", "one pager", "visual summary" = this section, never a deck. **Every picture in this section (thumbnails, preview, result) is a PNG printed by `infographic.py` or `onepager.py` and shown with `show()`. Never use the image generation tool, draw a chart in Python, reproduce a vendor diagram or show a picture you made yourself - not even as a "concept" or "preview"; that is not the Ulta template.** A request that already names the design ("product one-pager", "status one-pager option 2") skips steps 1-2: go straight to step 3 with that id (`infographic.py show` maps numbers to ids). Topic knowledge (e.g. what a system function does) goes into the answers as text, never as a picture. Format: one-pager unless the user says slide. Here you DO ask (this overrides "don't ask first"), one step per message, and build only when step 3 is answered:
1. **Purpose** - `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/infographic.py start` and ask its question ("What is the one-pager for? 1 weekly/project status ... 5 a story told with your pictures, 6 something else"). Skip it if the request already says ("weekly status one-pager" = status; "one-pager with my screenshots" = pictures).
2. **Pick a design - generate and show the skill's official thumbnails in the chat before asking.** Never search the web, show stock or third-party templates (PowerSlides, SlideModel, Canva), draw an unofficial mock-up, replace the thumbnails with a text list, or ask whether to use a classic layout. Use the same renderer that builds the one-pager so the choices always match the actual templates.
```python
r = subprocess.run([sys.executable, f'{SKILL_DIR}/tools/infographic.py', 'show', '--group', 'status',
                    '--out', '/mnt/data/out/designs.png'], capture_output=True, text=True)
print(r.stdout, r.stderr[-300:])
show('/mnt/data/out/designs.png')
```
   The picture must appear in the chat above your question (that is what `show` does). In the reply also add the link `[Open the designs](sandbox:/mnt/data/out/designs.png)`, then ask only: "Which design - 1, 2, 3 or 4?" (numbers as printed on the picture and in the tool's list; use the tool's list to map the number to the template id). Do not go on until the user picks. If `show` printed SHOW FAILED, give the link plus the tool's numbered list (name and one line each) and ask the same question.
3. **The facts** - run `infographic.py ask <id> --out /mnt/data/out/preview.png`, put it in the chat with `show('/mnt/data/out/preview.png')` in the same cell (the chosen design, larger), then send its numbered questions in ONE message (they may also paste notes or upload a file). If answers are missing, ask once for just those; still missing = "-". Never invent facts and never build with the template's sample content (the build FAILs if you do).
4. **Build and deliver** - write the answers into the template, replacing every sample line, then build:
```
python {SKILL_DIR}/modules/ultabranddeliverables/scripts/ulta.py content-template status > c.md
python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/onepager.py c.md --template status [--format slide] [--theme glow] --out /mnt/data/out/<Name>
```
   **A one-pager is one page:** the portrait one-pager, or with `--format slide` one 16:9 slide on the template - no cover, no closing slide. Add the template's cover and closing slides (`--frame`) only when the user asks for them. A status report too long for one slide at a readable size continues on a "(cont.)" slide: say so, and offer the portrait one-pager, which holds more.
   Show the result in the chat (`show('/mnt/data/out/<Name>.png')`; if no PNG was made, skip it), attach the .pptx and PDF, and offer another theme (`infographic.py themes`, displayed the same way) or design as a one-command rebuild.
If the user pasted their content with the request, skip the questions: pick the design from the content (or `--template auto`) and build.
All designs at once: `infographic.py show`. Themes: `infographic.py themes`. Every picture these print (`PICTURE …`) is for the chat: `show()` it before asking for a selection. Design pictures come only from these tools or bundled assets - never from the web.

**The look:** a light orange lead on a light page, unless the user picks another theme: the big shapes - a journey's stages (steps, stairs, arrows, layers, rings, the curve), the message banner, a hub's centre, a house's roof and foundation - run from Ulta's light orange into Ulta orange, each stage a little deeper, with dark words; the small marks - the discs behind pictograms, badges, section marks - take the brand's colours in turn (Ulta orange, raspberry, magenta, berry, red-orange), each running into a deeper shade of itself, with white pictograms (a status colour stays its own). Two other looks on the Ulta theme: `"look": "gradient"` (deep: Ulta orange into red-orange and raspberry, white words) and `"look": "flat"` (the colours as they are, as Ulta's printed one-pagers); the page has a Light / Deep / Flat switch. A slide sits on the template's own content slide and keeps its colour theme - the cream background and the template's colours (the build's `Template look kept` check fails if anything writes over it); the picture and the page show the same cream.

**Make your own** (shape -> colours -> blocks): `infographic.py blocks` lists the 24 blocks (big numbers, steps, timeline, points, bar chart, rings, status strip, before & after, table, quote, message, picture, picture & points, pictures, value band, today & tomorrow, and the ways to tell a story below). With the user's content, choose the blocks yourself (up to 8, one message per block, only their numbers), write `spec.json` and build; without content, show the block list and ask which to include. An item may name its pictogram (`"icon": "truck"`); `"half": true` on two blocks in a row puts them side by side.

**Ways to tell a story** - the same content in another shape, all on the Ulta theme: a journey as **climbing steps** (`stairs`: each stage a step higher, a flag on the top step), **arrows** (`chevrons`), a **pyramid** (layers that build up, the first at the base), a **rising curve** (`curve`: milestones on an S-curve) or **widening rings** (`rings`: each stage a wider circle around the last) - items `{when, title, text}`, `when` a short label such as "Stage 01"; one idea and its parts as a **hub** (`center` + 2-8 items); a vision on pillars as a **house** (`roof` the vision, 1-6 pillars, `base` the message); points as **tiles** (`cards`). When the user asks for other ways to show a story ("instead of 1, 2, 3", "other options", "a deck with all the options"): `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/infographic.py ways spec.json --out /mnt/data/out/<Name>-ways [--look gradient]` - one deck, the template's cover then one slide per way (its journey as each journey shape, its points as tiles or around a hub, a house; nothing added or left out), and `<Name>-ways-ways.png` with all of them numbered: `show()` it, attach the deck and ask which one; `make <Name>-ways-<n>.json --out /mnt/data/out/<Name>` builds the pick on its own, in any format. In the page, a block's Type switches it in place with its words kept (steps become stairs, arrows or a pyramid; a timeline keeps its dates).
```
python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/infographic.py make spec.json --out /mnt/data/out/<Name> [--format slide|square] [--theme velvet] [--frame]
```
A slide is the slide alone; `--frame` (asked for only) adds the template's cover and closing slides.
`{"title":"…","subtitle":"…","format":"onepager","theme":"ulta","blocks":[{"type":"stats","title":"…","items":[["42%","what it means"]]},{"type":"steps","items":[["Plan","…"]]},{"type":"takeaway","text":"…"}]}`
`<Name>-spec.json` is saved: another theme or shape is the same command with `--theme`/`--format` (no rewrite). The deliverable is the **.pptx** (editable native shapes), with its .png to show in the chat and a .pdf handout; the engine also writes .svg and .html working files, which this PowerPoint skill does not offer as deliverables. A one-pager the user changed in PowerPoint goes through `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/fix_deck.py` like any edited deck.

## Stories with pictures - the user's screenshots, mock-ups and product photos
Product teams tell many one-pagers around pictures: a UI concept for a budget ask, the product for an introduction, what shipped for a status update. The pictures are always the ones the user uploaded - never searched, generated, drawn by you or taken from the web (this skill has no UI screens tool: when there is no screen yet, ask for a sketch or screenshot). The designs' sample pictures (`sample:`) are for the thumbnails only: the build FAILs when one is left in.
- **Designs:** `infographic.py show --group pictures` (budget proposal with a UI concept, product introduction, status update with screenshots), then `infographic.py ask <id>` as in steps 2-3: its questions say which pictures to upload and what to write.
- **Content:** a picture is a Markdown picture line under its section heading: `![what it shows](/mnt/data/<file>.png)`. The caption is printed under the picture and describes it for screen readers: write what it shows. One picture with points = the picture beside its points; one alone = a picture; 2 to 4 = side by side. A section with `### Today` and `### Tomorrow` steps = how the work changes, in two lanes. Free-form content (`--template free`) reads picture lines the same way.
- **What the build does:** every picture is read and drawn again - turned upright, at most 1,800 px, without its metadata (camera, place, date, comments). PNG, JPEG, WebP or GIF; at most 15 MB each and 12 on a page. A screenshot gets a browser frame (a tall one, a phone), a photo none. Pictures that do not fit are left out with a warning: tell the user which.
- **The Pictures check:** FAIL when a picture is missing or is a sample; REVIEW for screenshots - when you deliver, ask the user to check that no guest, customer or confidential data shows before sharing (text inside a picture is not scanned).
- **Make your own:** `image`, `feature` (picture & points), `gallery`, `band` (the value in one strip) and `flow` (today & tomorrow); a picture's `"src"` is the uploaded file.

**Readable text:** every word is judged on what it sits on, in the colours the files get: 4.5:1, or 3:1 from 18 pt (bold earns no allowance: a viewer without the template's font draws it regular). Words on a colour people see as dark are light: on the Ulta theme the marks without words (badges, icon discs, section marks) are the brand's colours running into a deeper shade, with white pictograms; the light orange lead carries dark words (a stage name, the message), marked to stay dark wherever dark reads; the cards that carry words (big numbers, table heads, the value band, tiles) are pale orange with dark words; the deep look puts white words of 14 pt bold or more on its orange-to-raspberry run; sunset's cards run gold into a pastel with dark words; tinted zones and quadrants keep dark titles. A word that would still fall short takes white, a deeper template colour of its own hue (raspberry for the pinks) or black - an orange word turns black, never plum - whichever reads first, and the rest of its card follows, so a card never mixes white and black. Each build records it as the `Readable text` check (FAIL names the text). Never recolour text yourself.

**Standalone diagram:** `python {SKILL_DIR}/tools/onepager.py --diagram flows.txt --style zones --title "…" --out /mnt/data/out/<Name>`. Point graphics go in a deck as `{"graphic":"funnel","headline":"…","items":["Label: detail",…]}` (funnel, pyramid, stairs, milestones, quadrant, venn, target, honeycomb). Pictures of everything: `modules/ultabranddeliverables/references/gallery/`.
